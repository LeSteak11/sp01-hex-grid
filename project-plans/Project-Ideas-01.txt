Category 1: Urban & Civic Analytics (The Core GIS Heavyweights)
These projects prove you can handle standard municipal data but elevate it far beyond a typical government portal.

1. The 15-Minute City Hex-Grid

The Concept: A map evaluating how accessible groceries, parks, and transit are within a 15-minute walk from any point in the city.

The Data: OpenStreetMap (OSM) via the Overpass API for amenities, and municipal GeoJSON for street networks.

The UX Hook: Instead of messy points, aggregate the data into a sleek, glowing 3D hex-grid (using deck.gl) that users can hover over to see walkability scores.

2. Historical Redlining vs. Modern Heat Islands

The Concept: Visualizing the lasting environmental impact of 1930s housing policies on today's neighborhood temperatures.

The Data: HOLC shapefiles (University of Richmond’s Mapping Inequality project) and Landsat satellite surface temperature data.

The UX Hook: A smooth split-screen slider allowing users to drag back and forth between historical parchment-style maps and modern thermal data.

3. The Gentrification & Building Lifecycle Timeline

The Concept: An interactive map showing the age and evolution of building footprints to visualize urban density over time.

The Data: "Building Footprints" datasets (usually rich GeoJSON or Shapefiles on city open data portals) containing year_built attributes.

The UX Hook: A timeline slider at the bottom. As the user drags it, buildings animate and extrude into 3D existence, color-coded by zoning type.

4. Noise Pollution 3D Topography

The Concept: Mapping a city's 311 noise complaints as a topographic mountain range rather than a flat heatmap.

The Data: Municipal 311 service request API endpoints (filtering for noise/party/construction complaints).

The UX Hook: Render the density of complaints as smooth, stylized 3D elevation peaks. The louder the area, the higher the "mountain."

5. Real-Time Transit Isochrones

The Concept: A map where the user clicks anywhere, and a fluid, animated blob expands to show exactly how far they can travel on public transit within 30 minutes.

The Data: Real-time GTFS (General Transit Feed Specification) data and routing APIs.

The UX Hook: Micro-interactions. The transit polygon should pulse gently, with smooth easing animations as the user changes the time slider from 15 to 45 minutes.

Category 2: Aesthetic & Experimental (Flexing the Design Background)
These ideas merge custom graphic design styles with spatial data, proving you know how to manipulate the absolute core of map styling engines.

6. Synthwave Night-Time Transit Tracker

The Concept: A live map of public transit vehicles moving across the city, styled entirely in an 80s retro-futuristic aesthetic.

The Data: Live GTFS-realtime vehicle position feeds.

The UX Hook: Deep dark backgrounds, glowing neon pink and cyan vector lines for routes, and custom MapLibre JSON styling to completely overhaul the basemap.

7. 16-Bit Pixel Art Neighborhood Renderer

The Concept: A hyper-stylized map that renders standard city blocks to look like an old-school top-down RPG.

The Data: Standard OpenStreetMap vector tiles.

The UX Hook: Strip back all traditional map rendering and use custom icons, blocky fonts, and pixelated texture fills for water, grass, and concrete.

8. Hex-Based Resource Mapping

The Concept: Visualize a city's power grid, water lines, or internet infrastructure by aggregating the data into discrete hexes. Style it heavily like a modern strategy game (think Civilization VI), where hexes represent resource density.

The Data: Municipal infrastructure line data (GeoJSON) and Turf.js to calculate intersections within the hexes.

The UX Hook: High contrast, gamified UI panels, and 3D extrusion based on "resource yield" (infrastructure density).

9. Fictional City Procedural Generator

The Concept: Build a world-building tool that uses spatial algorithms to generate fictional street networks and zoning districts over real topography.

The Data: Real digital elevation models (DEM) combined with Turf.js for procedural geometry generation.

The UX Hook: Allow users to export the resulting hybrid city maps as GeoJSON files, complete with auto-generated names for distinct municipal wards.

10. Cinematic "Scrollytelling" StoryMap

The Concept: A scroll-driven narrative map (similar to The New York Times graphics desk) detailing a specific local crisis, like a severe storm's path or a housing shortage.

The Data: Historical weather paths or census tract housing data.

The UX Hook: As the user scrolls down the webpage text, the map pinned in the background automatically flies to new coordinates, zooms, and changes data layers smoothly.

Category 3: Environmental & Ecological
11. Wildfire Smoke & Wind Particle Flow

The Concept: A living map showing real-time wind patterns carrying wildfire smoke or urban pollution.

The Data: NOAA weather APIs, EPA Air Quality Index (AQI) sensor data.

The UX Hook: Using WebGL to render thousands of moving, fading wind particles flowing across a minimalist, monochromatic basemap.

12. Coastal Flood Risk Simulator

The Concept: An interactive tool to visualize which properties go underwater as sea levels rise.

The Data: High-resolution coastal DEM (elevation) data and municipal property boundary polygons.

The UX Hook: A menacing, fluid blue overlay that rises and covers property parcels as the user drags a "Water Level" slider up.

13. Urban Tree Canopy Network

The Concept: Mapping the exact species, health, and location of every registered tree in a city to highlight ecological equity.

The Data: City Parks & Recreation "Tree Inventory" CSVs/APIs.

The UX Hook: Style the data points less like dots and more like a glowing, interconnected biological network, using color gradients to represent tree age or canopy size.

Category 4: Infrastructure & Human Flow
14. Broadband Access & Digital Redlining

The Concept: Visualizing the layout of fiber optic internet lines against household income to show disparities in digital access.

The Data: FCC broadband mapping data and US Census demographic APIs.

The UX Hook: A stark, high-contrast map where the underlying network infrastructure pulses like a circuit board, contrasting sharply with data deserts.

15. Maritime Shipping & Supply Chain Arcs

The Concept: A global or regional map showing the volume and flow of cargo ships between ports.

The Data: AIS (Automatic Identification System) vessel tracking data (historical or live).

The UX Hook: A 3D globe (using MapLibre's globe projection) with glowing Sankey-style arcs leaping across oceans, visually weighted by the tonnage of cargo.
